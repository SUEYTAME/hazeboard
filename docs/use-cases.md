# Ideas for using Hazeboard

Hazeboard puts small glass panels of notes on your desktop. Because they are
painted into your wallpaper, you see them every time you glance at your desktop,
even when Hazeboard is closed. That makes it good for things you want to *keep
seeing*, not things you want to file away.

Open your board with **Ctrl+Alt+W** (or by clicking the Hazeboard icon in the
taskbar tray), make a panel by typing in **New panel…**, and press **Esc** when
you're done. Missed the tutorial? Right-click the tray icon → **Show tutorial**.

---

## Today's to-do list

One panel with what has to happen today. Tick things off as you go; the bottom
of the panel counts how many are done.

> - Send the invoice to Ana
> - Pay the electricity bill
> - Pick up the package before 6

**Tip:** keep it to five or six notes. A panel you can read at a glance is one
you'll actually look at.

## Shopping list

Add items the moment you notice you're out of something, and check the panel
before you leave.

> - Coffee
> - Eggs
> - Dish soap

**Tip:** give it its own colour (right-click a note → pick a colour) so you can
find it on your desktop without reading.

## Study plan

Students: one panel per subject, or one panel for the week before an exam.

> - Exam week:
> - Chapter 4 exercises
> - Review the lab report
> - Ask the professor about question 7

**Tip:** a first note like *"Exam week:"* works as a label, so you can tell
your panels apart at a glance.

## Work: doing and waiting-on

Two panels side by side: what you're doing, and what you're waiting for from
other people. The second one is the list people forget to keep.

> - Doing:
> - Finish the budget draft
> - Prepare Thursday's slides

> - Waiting on:
> - Carlos: signed contract
> - IT: new laptop

**Tip:** when something you were waiting on arrives, move it. Hold the board
until it wiggles, then drag the note from one panel into the other.

## Reminders you'll see when you sit down

Because the panels are part of the wallpaper, they're there the moment you log
in, before you open any app.

> - Take medicine at 8 pm
> - Water the plants on Sunday
> - Mom's birthday on the 14th

## A family or shared computer

A panel of chores or messages everyone sees on the shared PC.

> - Take out the recycling (Tuesday)
> - Buy printer ink
> - Book the car service

## Ideas parking lot

Somewhere to drop a thought quickly so it doesn't interrupt what you're doing.
Clean it up once a week.

> - Weekend trip to the mountains?
> - Birthday present for Luis
> - Try the new pasta recipe

## Tips

- **Colours mean what you want them to.** For example: red for urgent, green
  for personal, blue for work. Right-click a note to pick one; the slider sets
  how strong it is.
- **Move panels around.** Hold a panel for half a second until the board
  wiggles, then drag it. Click empty space when you're done.
- **Make a panel from a note.** While the board wiggles, drag a note out into
  empty space and it becomes a panel of its own.
- **Keep panels away from your desktop icons.** The right-hand side of the
  screen is usually free.
- **Remove things.** While the board wiggles, use the small **×** on a note or
  on a panel.
- **Mind what you write.** Your panels are part of your wallpaper, so anyone
  who can see your desktop — including people watching you share your whole
  screen — can read them. Don't put passwords or private details on them.
